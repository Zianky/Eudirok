Dubai and the UAE gold market

How the UAE became one of the world’s main physical gold markets, and the standards that apply to gold traded there.

A global hub

Dubai is one of the world’s main physical gold markets. DMCC, the free zone created in 2002 to build Dubai’s commodities trade, reports more than 25,000 member companies. It says Dubai accounts for about 15% of worldwide gold trade, and that the majority of it flows through DMCC.

A 2022 report on the UAE by Metals Focus for the LBMA put the UAE at about 10% of world gold bullion exports and about 10% of world gold jewellery exports in 2020, third in the world behind mainland China and Hong Kong, and Switzerland. Rankings and shares differ by year and by how trade is measured, so each figure here is given with its source.

Standards used in the UAE

The Dubai Good Delivery standard was introduced in 2005 for 1 kg gold bars of 995 fineness. In November 2021 the UAE announced the UAE Good Delivery (UAEGD) standard, which replaces it nationwide. Adherence to UAEGD is voluntary.

LBMA Good Delivery status is separate. It applies to refiners on the LBMA Good Delivery List and to their eligible bars. Our buyer’s guide explains what the LBMA and LPPM specifications require.

Since January 2023 the Ministry of Economy’s due diligence regulations for responsible sourcing of gold have applied to gold refineries operating in the UAE. Read how they work.

Storage, trading and events

The DMCC Vault in Almas Tower, Jumeirah Lakes Towers, is owned by DMCC and operated by Brink’s Global Services. The Dubai Gold and Commodities Exchange (DGCX) trades gold contracts.

The Dubai Precious Metals Conference takes place on 16 and 17 November 2026.
