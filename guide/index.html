Buyer’s guide to precious metals

Three things worth knowing before you buy or sell physical gold, and how silver, platinum and palladium bars differ: how purity is stated, how weight is counted and what paperwork to ask for.

How purity is stated

Purity, or fineness, is the number of parts of pure gold in a thousand. A bar marked 999.9 is 99.99% gold, often called “four nines”. Jewellery is usually described in carats, out of 24. So 24 carat is the purest, 22 carat is about 916 parts per thousand and 18 carat is 750.

The bar’s stamp should show its fineness, a serial number and the refiner’s mark. LBMA Good Delivery bars made since January 2019 also show the month and year of manufacture. Ask for all of them.

How weight is counted

Precious metals are weighed in troy ounces, not ordinary ounces. One troy ounce is 31.1035 grams, so a one kilogram bar holds about 32.15 troy ounces.

The large bars traded between banks in London are called Good Delivery bars. The London Bullion Market Association (LBMA) sets their rules: between 350 and 430 troy ounces of fine gold, which is roughly 10.9 to 13.4 kilograms, with a minimum fineness of 995.0 parts per thousand. Only refiners on the LBMA’s Good Delivery List make bars that qualify.

Silver, platinum and palladium

Silver bars in the London market weigh about 1,000 troy ounces, roughly 32 kilograms, with a tolerance of 10% either way, and must be at least 999.0 parts per thousand silver. Each bar carries a serial number, the refiner’s stamp, its fineness and the month and year of manufacture.

Platinum and palladium follow rules set by the London Platinum and Palladium Market (LPPM). Plates and ingots weigh between 1 and 6 kilograms and must be at least 99.95% pure. They are marked with a serial number, the refiner’s assay stamp, the fineness, the metal name, the month and year of manufacture and the weight. The LPPM Good Delivery List names the refiners whose plates and ingots are accepted across the market.

Gold (LBMA)
350 to 430 fine oz, min. 995.0
Silver (LBMA)
About 1,000 oz, min. 999.0
Platinum (LPPM)
1 to 6 kg, min. 99.95%
Palladium (LPPM)
1 to 6 kg, min. 99.95%

Sources: LBMA Good Delivery rules and governance; LPPM Good Delivery rules.

What paperwork to ask for

  • The refiner’s name, and whether it appears on a recognised list such as the LBMA Good Delivery List
  • An assay certificate or other proof of fineness for the exact bar, matching its serial number
  • Proof that the seller owns the metal and may lawfully sell and export it
  • An invoice and delivery or storage documents that name the buyer and the seller
